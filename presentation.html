You pay half the value. You get a clean, debt-free half.

2

SB uses the proceeds to settle the existing bonds on Beachside and Menlyn. African Sky is already unbonded.

How leverage works

Using bricks to borrow, to buy more bricks.

This is the single idea behind the growth engine, and it's simpler than it sounds.

1

You own a property outright. Say NewCo owns a R6m property with no bond on it. That's R6m of equity just sitting there.

2

A bank lends against it — cheaply. Because the property is the security, a bank will advance around 60% of its value (R3.6m) at an ordinary home-loan rate. You still own the property and still collect its rent.

3

You use that cash as a deposit. Add the borrowed R3.6m to rental profit you've saved, and buy another income property. One property has become two — both earning.

4

The rent pays the loan. The new property's rent covers the repayments, and both properties keep rising in value. You've grown the portfolio using the bank's money, secured by bricks — not more of your own cash.

What the bank sees

Your equity 40%Bank bond 60%
A R6m property → R3.6m of low-cost borrowing power unlocked, R2.4m equity retained.
The catch, stated plainly: leverage cuts both ways. Debt must be serviced whether or not a property is fully let, and values can fall as well as rise. The engine below keeps gearing conservative (under ~25% of portfolio value) precisely so the rent always covers the loans with room to spare.
The growth engine

Three properties into six — a scenario.

Here's the flywheel, then a concrete illustration: NewCo buys one more income property every four years, each funded ~60% by a bank bond and ~40% from saved-up rental profit. Same four steps, each turn bigger than the last.

1 · Own

NewCo owns income property, mostly unbonded — equity building.

2 · Borrow

Bond ~60% against that equity at a low bank rate — releasing cash.

3 · Buy

Add saved rental profit and buy the next income property.

4 · Grow → repeat

New rent + value enlarge the equity base — so the next turn is bigger.
